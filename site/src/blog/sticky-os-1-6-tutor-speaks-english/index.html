{{NAME}} 1.6: Tutor speaks English

Tutor came in 1.5 with 7th grade geometry in Romanian. In 1.6 every lesson, exercise, hint, wrong-answer explanation and solution is in English too, and Tutor picks the language the board already speaks.

The board’s language decides

There is no setting for it. With the board in Romanian, Tutor is in Romanian. In English, or in any of the board’s other languages, its lessons and exercises are in English. They are never half one language and half the other: a language either has all of Tutor’s words or Tutor doesn’t use it. The buttons and headers around the lessons are ordinary board strings, so they follow the board’s language wherever they are translated, like the rest of {{NAME}}.

Change the language in Settings with an exercise open, and the same exercise comes back in the new language: the same numbers, the same right answer, and the answers you already crossed out. An exercise is made from a seed, and nothing but its words depends on the language.

What a lesson is

Each lesson starts with a summary of a few pages with figures, in plain words. Three worked examples then show the reasoning step by step. Then come six to eight exercises, each with four answers.

The wrong answers aren’t random: each is a mistake pupils really make, like adding the legs of a right triangle instead of multiplying them. Pick one and Tutor says which mistake it was. Hints gives up to two, written to point the way without giving the answer away, and Solution shows the whole working when you want it. A lesson keeps your best score, counted as the answers right the first time.

The nine lessons

Tutor is organised like school, a class, then a subject, then lessons. For now that is 7th grade maths, geometry. The unit numbers follow the Romanian textbook the lessons were written from:

  • Unit 4, the quadrilateral: 4.1 The convex quadrilateral, 4.2 The parallelogram, 4.3 The midsegment, 4.4 The rectangle, 4.5 The rhombus and the square, 4.6 The trapezoid, 4.7 Perimeter and area, and 4.8 Unit 4 review;
  • 7.2 The Pythagorean theorem.

How it was done

Until now, Tutor’s Romanian was written into its code. In 1.6 every word moved into tables by language, the way the rest of {{NAME}} keeps its ten languages: for each language, a table of the exercises’ words and a table of the lesson pages. The exercise generators take a language and use whole sentences where words used to be glued together, and nothing they decide depends on the language.

The Romanian didn’t change: it was moved word for word, and a full dump of every kind of exercise, at every level and over 1,500 seeds, came out byte for byte the same as before. Tutor’s tests now run on GitHub for every pull request and every change to the main branch: every exercise in every complete language, the same seed giving the same exercise in Romanian and English, every placeholder and letter checked, and the worked examples’ numbers checked in each language.

Teach it another language

A third language is mostly translation. Copy the Romanian tables, translate all of them (a language Tutor only half has is never shown), and run the tests, which make every exercise in the new language and fail if a translation doesn’t fit or makes a different exercise from the English. The steps and the rules (keep every placeholder, the language’s own notation, hints that add no numbers of their own) are under Tutor’s languages in CONTRIBUTING.md. If you teach 7th grade, a read of the English lesson text is just as welcome.

Also in 1.6

  • Fixed: the board could restart right after joining WiFi, when the clock’s time servers and the first weather lookup asked for addresses at the same moment. The time servers are now looked up first, on joining.
