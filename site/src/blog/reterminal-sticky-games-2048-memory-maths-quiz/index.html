reTerminal Sticky games: 2048, Memory, maths, quiz and more

E-paper is slow to redraw, so its games have to be calm ones: turn by turn, nothing that needs a quick hand. {{NAME}} has a folder of eight made for that, from 2048 to a geometry tutor, for a minute at the fridge or a longer sit at the table.

The Games folder and its controls

Games on the home screen is a folder: it opens its own grid of games, each with a line of how you’re doing, such as your best, your level or your daily streak. Tap a game to play; Back, top left, returns to the grid, and the status bar goes home.

The three buttons work too. UP and DOWN move a ring from game to game, and inside a game from button to button; OK presses the ringed one. Sideways swipes stay in the game rather than changing app. A game left half-played, such as 2048, X and O, a quiz or a story, is where you left it when you come back.

The eight games

2048

Swipe on the board, or press UP and DOWN, to slide every tile; two the same merge. Tiles get bolder as they grow, white on black from 128. New game starts again, and your best score is kept.

Memory

Easy, with 12 cards, or Hard, with 20. Tap two cards: a pair stays up, and a miss stays up until your next tap, since e-paper doesn’t flash them back for free. Fewest moves wins, and the best for each size is kept.

Maths Quest

Ten levels, from adding within 100 to fractions and the order of operations. Each has eight questions and a boss, three hearts, streaks and quick answers that score more, up to three stars, and the next level opens when you pass. A wrong answer is struck through and gets a hint, never the answer. Questions you miss wait in Practice until you get them right first time.

Daily maths

Five mixed questions, the same all day because they come from the date, and a streak of days in a row.

Quiz

Pick a theme (Science, Space, Nature, Geography, History, Words and numbers) or type your own, and the AI writes eight questions on it, four answers each and a fact to read after. Without the AI, the board’s own 48 questions play instead, and the screen says why. A mix is always the board’s own.

Adventure

Pick a world (pirates, space, a castle) or type your own idea, and the AI tells a story a scene at a time. You choose what happens next from two or three ways, and it ends happily after about eight scenes. The Lighthouse Key is a story built into the board, with its own turns and ending, for when there’s no AI.

X and O

You are X against the board, at Easy, Medium, Hard or Best, which can’t be beaten, only drawn. Who starts takes turns, and your wins, draws and losses are kept.

Tutor

A school tutor in Romanian and English, contributed to the project: 7th grade geometry, with a summary, three worked examples and 6 to 8 exercises per lesson. A wrong answer says why, and the hints never give the answer away. Tutor follows the board’s language: Romanian on a board set to Romanian, English on any other. The 1.5 post has the whole story, and the 1.6 post how it learned English.

The AI is optional

Only Quiz and Adventure use the AI, and only with your own OpenAI key, WiFi, and AI in games on in Settings. Without any of these they play their built-in questions and story, and nothing is sent. While the AI writes, the screen says so, with Cancel; if it fails, the screen says why in plain words and offers the built-in ones.

Progress, and hiding games

Best scores, levels, stars, the daily streak and the X and O tally are kept in the board’s own flash, along with up to 30 maths questions to practise. Settings → Home screen hides the Games folder, or any game in it.
