Your Google Calendar on an e-paper fridge display, no subscription

A family calendar belongs where everyone walks past it. With {{NAME}} on a reTerminal Sticky, the fridge door shows what’s on now, what’s next and the week ahead, read straight from Google, iCloud or Outlook. There is no account to make and nothing to pay for.

How it works, and why there is no subscription

Google Calendar, iCloud and Outlook can each give a calendar a secret address in iCal format: a link that lets anything holding it read the calendar. You paste that link into the {{NAME}} setup app, and the board reads it itself, every 30 minutes and whenever you press OK in the Calendar, over https, straight from the calendar service. Nothing goes through anyone else, so there is no service to sign up for and nothing to pay. {{NAME}} itself is free and open source.

The calendar on the board is read only: you add and change events on your phone or computer as usual, and the board shows them at its next read.

Step 1: copy the secret iCal address

Google Calendar

On a computer: Settings → pick the calendar under Settings for my calendars → Integrate calendar → Secret address in iCal format. Copy it.

iCloud

In the Calendar app, the calendar’s share button → turn on Public Calendar → copy the link. A link that starts webcal:// is fine.

Outlook and Microsoft 365

Settings → Calendar → Shared calendars → Publish a calendar, with Can view all details → copy the ICS link.

Treat the link as a password to that calendar. The board keeps it and never sends it back, and the setup app only ever shows the labels. If a link leaks, reset it in the calendar service and paste the new one.

Step 2: paste it into the setup app

Open the setup app in Chrome or Edge, connect to the board over USB or Bluetooth, and open Calendar. Paste up to three links, one per line, each with a label first if you like, such as Work https://…. Three is enough for, say, your own calendar, a partner’s and a shared one for the family. Save, and the board restarts and reads them.

If {{NAME}} isn’t on your board yet, install it first; it takes a few minutes over a USB cable.

What the fridge door shows

The Calendar app is made to be read from across the room. At the top, a black card shows the event on now, until when and how long is left, or what’s next: “Free until 14:00”, “in 25 min”. In the evening it says “Nothing else today” and shows tomorrow’s first event.

Under it is the week, Monday first, with today in a black circle and a dot under each day with events still to come. Then the agenda: a heading for each day with that day’s forecast high and low, and a row for each event. A bar marks the one on now.

  • Swipe up or down for the agenda’s pages.
  • Tap a day in the week to jump to it.
  • Tap an event to see all of it: title, day, time, place, and which calendar it came from.
  • OK goes back to today and reads the calendars again.

Held upright, the card, the week and the agenda stack; turned sideways, the card and the week sit on the left and the agenda on the right. Home shows the next event too, beside the time and the weather.

Repeats, time zones and long histories

Repeating events are worked out on the board: daily, weekly, monthly and yearly, every few days or weeks, a number of times or until a date, “the 2nd Tuesday” or “the last Friday”, with dates left out and single instances moved or cancelled. Times follow the calendar’s own time zone, so a 9:00 meeting stays at 9:00 across a change to or from summer time. Cancelled events don’t show.

The board reads the feed as it arrives and keeps only yesterday to 30 days ahead, so a calendar with years of history is fine. A feed over 8 MB, or one that takes more than 2 minutes to arrive, is refused. It keeps at most 200 events, the soonest first, and the same event in two calendars shows once.

A few rules aren’t read yet: repeats by week number, by day of the year or by the hour show their first time only, and “this and all future” changes aren’t followed.

When a calendar can’t be read

Each calendar keeps the events it had, and the foot of the screen says why in plain words, such as “Calendar link rejected (404) - check the address.” One calendar failing doesn’t hide the others, and the board tries again after 1, 2, 4 and up to 30 minutes.
