reTerminal Sticky e-reader: EPUB, MOBI and PDF from a microSD card

With {{NAME}}, the reTerminal Sticky reads books from a microSD card: EPUB, Kindle’s MOBI and AZW3 without DRM, FB2, PDF and text. This guide covers putting books on the card, what it reads, and how to get around a book.

What it reads

  • EPUB 2 and 3, the usual format of free and bought e-books;
  • MOBI, AZW and AZW3, Kindle’s formats, when they have no DRM;
  • FB2, plain text (.txt, .md) and HTML;
  • PDF, as its text, reflowed to the screen. A PDF page shrunk to 3.97 inches would be too small to read, so the board shows the words instead. Most text PDFs read well; a scanned PDF has no text to show, and a page with columns, tables or formulas may come out in the wrong order.

Copy-protected books (DRM: most bought from Amazon, Kobo, Google or Apple) can’t be read, and the board says so. Free books without DRM come from Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks.

Putting books on the card

  1. On a computer, make a folder named books at the top of a microSD card (FAT).
  2. Copy your books into it. Sub-folders are fine, two levels deep: an author’s folder, then a title’s, as Calibre lays them out, so a Calibre library works as it is. With no books folder, the board lists the books at the card’s top.
  3. Put the card in the board and open Books on the home screen.

The first time the app looks at a card it reads each book’s title as it goes (“Reading the card: 3 of 12”); after that the list comes at once. The first time you open a book, the board turns it into its own simple text and keeps that on the card. A novel takes a few seconds, behind a bar you can cancel; after that it opens at once.

If {{NAME}} isn’t on your board yet, see how to install it.

The library and a book’s page

The library opens with your reading time and how many days in a row you’ve read, then a card for the book you were reading: tap it to go straight on. Under it come Reading, New and Finished, each book with its author and format. Tap a book for its page: the cover, how far you are and in which chapter, how long you’ve read it, the time left once the board knows your pace, the book’s own description when it has one, and its chapters. Tap a chapter to open the book there.

Reading and typography

A book opens on its cover, in the panel’s four greys, then its pages take the whole screen: the chapter, the time and the battery at the top, and at the foot the pages or minutes left in the chapter. Tap the right side, swipe left or press DOWN for the next page; tap the left side, swipe right or press UP for the one before.

Tap the middle of the page for the menu: a progress bar with a mark for each chapter (tap it to jump), then Library, Contents, Bookmarks and Text. Text has seven sizes, from about 6 pt to 12.5 pt in print, three line spacings, three margins, and an even right edge on or off. The page changes as you choose, keeps your place, and the choices hold for every book.

Bookmarks, your place and statistics

Tap the top right corner of a page to add a bookmark (a ribbon shows there), and again to remove it. Bookmarks in the menu lists them with the chapter and the first words. Your place is kept on the card for every book, whatever the text size or way up, so the board opens on the page you left, even after a restart or sleep. A book read to the end moves to Finished.

Statistics show today’s and this week’s reading, the days in a row and your longest run, books finished, time and pages in all, and a bar for each of the last seven days. Time counts while a book is open and its pages turn; a page left alone for more than five minutes counts as one minute. The days need the clock, so WiFi.

What the board keeps on the card

Everything Books makes lives in a hidden folder, .sticky/books: the books ready to read, their covers, your places and bookmarks. You can delete it; the books are got ready again when opened, though your places and bookmarks go with it. When the card fills, the board removes the ready copies of finished books first.

Not there yet: hyphenation, search, and sending books from the setup app instead of the card. The 1.4 release post tells how the app was made, including the one line that made it fifteen times faster, and the guide has every detail. For a reader-only firmware, see the comparison.
