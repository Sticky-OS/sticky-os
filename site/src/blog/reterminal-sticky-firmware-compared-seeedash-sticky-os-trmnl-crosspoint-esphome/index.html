reTerminal Sticky firmware compared: Seeedash, {{NAME}}, TRMNL, CrossPoint and ESPHome

The reTerminal Sticky is an open board: Seeed lists a whole page of firmware for it, and you can flash one, try it, and go back. This is a fair look at the main options, what each is made for, and which to pick.

A note first: this blog belongs to {{NAME}}, so read it with that in mind. Everything said here about the other firmware comes from its own pages or Seeed’s, linked as we go, and each is good at what it is made for.

Seeedash, the firmware it ships with

The board comes with Seeed’s own firmware, v1.2.0 at the time of writing. Seeed’s product page says it has on-device apps for notes, to-do, clock, weather and images, and that you choose what shows through the screen or the Seeedash companion app for iOS and Android. CNX Software adds that it can show photos or monitor an AI agent, and that it speaks English and Simplified Chinese. It is the simplest choice if you want the board as Seeed designed it, with a phone app.

{{NAME}}

{{NAME}} gives the board a home screen of thirteen apps made to be read from across the room: clock, weather, a calendar from your iCal links, notes and voice notes, timer, photos in four greys, quote of the day, recipes, an e-reader and a folder of eight games. It speaks ten languages. You set it up from a web page over USB or Bluetooth; there is no account and no company server in the middle. AI is optional: voice notes, recipes and new quizzes use your own OpenAI key, and everything else works without one.

What it doesn’t do: it draws no Home Assistant dashboards (it can send its own sensor readings to an MQTT broker you run), and its e-reader has no hyphenation or search yet.

TRMNL

TRMNL’s firmware (GPL-3.0) wakes, fetches a ready-made screen from a server, draws it and sleeps again. The server does the work: TRMNL’s BYOD page lists the Sticky and speaks of more than 1,000 plugins. To use TRMNL’s own service on a board you bring, its docs describe a one-time licence per device, with a 14-day free trial, or you can skip the licence and run a server of your own (BYOS).

CrossPoint Reader

CrossPoint Reader is a community-built, open-source e-reader firmware that lists the reTerminal Sticky among its devices. It reads EPUB 2 and 3, XTC, plain text and BMP, with hyphenation, footnotes, bookmarks, dictionary look-ups, custom fonts, highlights, and files sent over WiFi or WebDAV. If reading is all you want the board for, it goes further as a reader than {{NAME}}’s Books app; {{NAME}} reads MOBI, AZW3, FB2 and PDF as well, beside its other apps.

ESPHome and OpenDisplay, for Home Assistant

ESPHome builds firmware from a YAML file; Seeed’s playground describes it on the Sticky as a customisable control panel for Home Assistant. You decide every pixel, and you need a Home Assistant set-up to make it worth it.

OpenDisplay is an open standard and open firmware (GPL-3.0) for e-paper screens: a sender renders a picture and the screen shows it, over Bluetooth Low Energy. Home Assistant is its reference sender. Its toolbox flashes the firmware from the browser.

Which to pick

  • Home Assistant at home: ESPHome if you like writing the layout yourself, OpenDisplay if you would rather send pictures from Home Assistant.
  • A dedicated reader: CrossPoint Reader.
  • Ready-made dashboards: TRMNL, for its plugin library, if a licence or a server of your own suits you.
  • A calm board for the kitchen, with no account: {{NAME}}: calendar, weather, notes, books, photos and games, set up from the browser.
  • The board as Seeed made it, with a phone app: keep Seeedash.

These are not the only ones. Seeed’s playground lists more partner and community firmware, from other e-readers to a pocket computer, a stream deck and a medication reminder.

Trying one is not a commitment

Each firmware is flashed over USB, and Seeed’s official web flasher puts Seeedash back whenever you like. To try {{NAME}}, see how to install it or the short version on the home page; to go back, see restore the Seeedash firmware.
