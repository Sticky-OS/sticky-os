{{NAME}} 1.5: Tutor, 7th grade geometry lessons in Romanian

The Games folder has a new member, and it came from a contributor. Tutor teaches 7th grade geometry, in Romanian, the way a patient tutor would: a short summary, worked examples, then exercises where a wrong answer is explained and a clue never gives the answer away.

What it teaches

Tutor is organised the way school is: a class, then a subject, then lessons, so more classes and subjects can follow. It starts with 7th grade maths, geometry: all of Unit 4, the quadrilateral (the convex quadrilateral, the parallelogram, the midline and the centroid, the rectangle, the rhombus and the square, the trapezoid, perimeters and areas, and a recap), and 7.2, the Pythagorean theorem.

Each lesson has three parts. A summary of three to five pages, with figures, follows what the textbooks teach, in plain words. Three worked examples show the reasoning step by step. Then come six to eight exercises, each with four answers.

Mistakes that teach

The wrong answers aren’t random numbers. Each one is a mistake pupils really make: forgetting to divide by two, subtracting from 90° instead of 180°, mixing up centimetres and decimetres. Pick one and Tutor says which mistake it was and why it’s wrong. Indicii gives up to two clues, written so they point the way without saying the answer, and Rezolvarea shows the whole solution once you want it. A lesson keeps your best score, counting answers right at the first try.

Exercises made from numbers, and checked twice

No exercise is typed by hand. Tutor picks the numbers, a solver works out the answer, the wrong options come from the typical mistakes, each with its own explanation, and a second check works the answer out another way before the exercise is ever shown. It also checks that there’s exactly one right option, that the options match the numbers, and that no clue gives the answer away. An exercise that fails is never shown. On a computer, about two and a half million checks run against twenty thousand seeds of every kind, so every pupil gets fresh exercises and none of them is wrong.

The fonts have no square root sign, so the screen draws one. The board has no figure files either: every triangle and quadrilateral is drawn from the exercise’s own numbers, with the sides it gives marked and what it asks marked with a question mark.

A contribution, tested on the board

Tutor was written by pushul and arrived as a pull request. Before it went in, it ran on a reTerminal Sticky, every lesson walked through, and the review turned up one thing worth telling: making an exercise used most of the main loop’s 8 KB stack, leaving under 1 KB spare. Exercises are now built in the board’s large PSRAM instead, and the loop keeps over 5 KB free. A few wrong-answer explanations were tightened too, so each mistake is explained by the reason that really produced it.

The lessons are in Romanian, whatever language the board speaks: that is who they were written for. If you teach 7th grade, a read of the lesson text is the most useful thing you could send.
