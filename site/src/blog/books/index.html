{{NAME}} 1.4: a books app for the fridge door

E-paper was made for reading, so the reTerminal Sticky now reads books. Put them on a microSD card and the board becomes a small e-reader, with justified serif pages, covers, contents, bookmarks and reading statistics.

What it reads

Copy books into a folder named books on a microSD card (sub-folders are fine, so a Calibre library works as it is) and open Books on the home screen. It reads:

  • EPUB 2 and 3, the usual format of free and bought e-books;
  • MOBI, AZW and AZW3, Kindle’s formats, when they have no DRM;
  • FB2, plain text, Markdown and HTML;
  • PDF, as its text, reflowed to the screen. A PDF page shrunk to 3.97 inches would be too small to read, so the board takes out the words, puts the paragraphs back together, drops the running headers and page numbers, and joins the hyphenated words. Books and documents read well; papers with columns, tables and formulas come out less tidy, and a scanned PDF has no text to show.

Copy-protected books can’t be read, and the board says so rather than showing garbage. Free books without DRM come from Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks.

The library and a book’s page

The library opens with your reading time and streak, then a card for the book you were reading: tap it and you’re back on your page. Under it come the books you’re reading, the new ones and the finished ones. Tap any book for its page: the cover, the publisher’s description when the book has one, how far you are and in which chapter, the time you’ve spent on it and, once the board knows your pace, the time left. Then all its chapters, the one you’re in marked: tap one to start there.

Reading

A book opens on its cover, in the panel’s four greys, then its pages take the whole screen: the chapter and the time at the top, how far you are at the foot. Tap the right of the page or swipe left for the next one, the left for the one before; UP and DOWN turn pages too. The type is GNU FreeSerif, in regular, italic, bold and bold italic, with the accents of the board’s ten languages and Cyrillic, justified, with the paragraphs indented as in a printed book.

Tap the middle of a page for the menu. It has the status bar, the title, the chapter, the page and a progress bar with a mark at each chapter: tap the bar to jump. Under it are the Library, the Contents, your Bookmarks (tap the page’s top right corner to add one) and Text: seven text sizes, three line spacings, three margins, and justified or ragged lines. The page changes as you choose, and your place stays where it was.

Your place, your bookmarks and your reading time are kept on the card for every book, so the board opens on the page you left, after a restart or a night asleep. Time counts while a book is open and its pages turn; a page left alone for more than five minutes counts as one minute.

Five short books to start with

The board in these pictures carries five books, each under 250 pages and free from Project Gutenberg, four of them by winners of the Nobel Prize in Literature:

  • Gitanjali, Rabindranath Tagore (Nobel 1913): the song offerings the prize was given for.
  • Siddhartha, Hermann Hesse (Nobel 1946): a life’s search for what matters, in a hundred and fifty pages.
  • Pygmalion, Bernard Shaw (Nobel 1925): how the way we speak decides how we’re seen.
  • The Jungle Book, Rudyard Kipling (Nobel 1907): belonging, law and loyalty, for any age.
  • Metamorphosis, Franz Kafka: what a family owes the one who can no longer provide.

Under the hood

Convert once, then read from the card

A book is turned into the app’s own simple text format the first time it is opened, and that copy is kept on the card with its contents and cover. A novel takes one to nine seconds on the board, behind a progress bar you can cancel; a long PDF takes longer. After that a book opens in about a twentieth of a second, at any page. A place in a book is a byte in that text, so it stays put whatever the text size or the way the board is held.

Pages that go back exactly

An e-reader lays out pages as you go, which makes going back harder than going forward. {{NAME}} lays pages out on a grid that depends only on the text and the layout: the book is cut into 32 KB stretches, each laid out from its own start. So the page before is always the exact page you saw, jumping to 90% of a long book lays out at most one stretch, and changing the text size and back returns you to the very same page. Turning a page lays it out in about a millisecond; the rest of the 0.6 seconds is the panel itself.

One line that made it fifteen times faster

The first build on the board opened a book in 0.77 seconds and spent as long on every text-size change. Timing each part on the board itself found it: the pager’s file was opened unbuffered, and on the ESP32 that makes every read of the card a byte at a time. With that one line gone, a book opens in 52 milliseconds.

Tight on memory, loose on flash

Everything a book needs lives in the board’s 8 MB of PSRAM: the read window, the page, the contents, the converters’ buffers. The whole app adds 88 bytes to the scarce internal RAM. The reading fonts, 30 of them at seven sizes and four styles, take about 730 KB of flash and no RAM. The readers are plain C++ that also builds on a computer, where thousands of tests run against real books in several languages and against files cut short or with random bytes flipped, so a damaged book shows an error rather than crashing the board.

Get it

{{NAME}} 1.4 is on GitHub Releases. On a board running {{NAME}}, open Settings → Software update, tap Check now, then Install. To install it the first time, see Install.

The guide has the details. Not there yet: hyphenation, search, and sending books from the setup app instead of the card. If a book doesn’t read well, an issue with where it came from helps.
